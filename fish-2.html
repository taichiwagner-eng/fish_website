Effects of Plastic

The effects of plastics on fish

Fish are not likely to ingest large pieces of plastic, however, they are more likely to ingest microplastics, which are microscopic plastic fragments. According to an article by EPA, “Plastic debris was found in 97% of the examined fish species, and almost a quarter of individual fish across the four locations in the South Pacific. Of the plastic debris extracted from the samples, 95% were microplastics (smaller than 5 mm).”4 The large plastics in the ocean can break down and become microplastics, which the fish are more prone to seeing as food. However, some larger fish may accidentally consume fish which have eaten plastic, called secondary ingestion.3 Fish that have ingested microplastics are at risk of many issues, such as plastic damaging cells or clogging gills, preventing oxygen intake. According to Environmentamerica, ”After a fish consumes microplastic, much of it stays in the GI tract and triggers false signals of fullness to the brain. Plastic can also cause intestinal obstruction or physical injury, which can lead to death.”2 If the fish unknowingly starve themselves to death, scavengers will eat plastic-infested fish, and also lessen the fish that can be consumed by predators and humans.

What happens to humans who eat the fish?

Humans who eat plastic through secondary or tertiary consumption are at risk of microplastics damaging cells or disrupting the immune system, both of which are also seen in fish that ate plastic.The article Microplastics in Fish and Fishery Products and Risks for Human Health: A Review stated, “Indeed, they can cause oxidative stress, cytotoxicity, and translocation to other tissues [94]. In organisms subjected to prolonged exposure, however, microplastics can lead to chronic flogosis, cell proliferation, necrosis, and impairment of immune cells.”1 The amount of total plastic ingestion also determines the danger the consumer is in, with more plastic having a larger effect.
